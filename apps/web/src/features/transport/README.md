# Transport

The bar in the footer: play and stop, the position readout, tempo, loop, count-in, metronome and master volume.

- **Commands first.** Every button and toggle runs a command from `commands.ts` through `useCommand`, so a click, its shortcut and the palette share one path, and tooltips and `aria-keyshortcuts` come from the registry. Sliders and the BPM field carry values and call the audio service directly; their discrete steps are commands.
- **Position never goes through React state.** The engine writes `playback.position` every animation frame. `startPositionReader` (`state/position-reader.ts`) coalesces that to one call per frame while playing and one per change otherwise, and the readout writes its spans' `textContent`. The playhead (WP-053, `app/playhead-driver.ts`) reads it this way; WP-074's fretboard should too.
- **Tempo is one percent factor** over the song's written tempo map (`audio.setTempo('percent', p)`). The BPM field is a view of it at the playhead: `round(written × p / 100)`, and typing a BPM sets `p` from the written tempo there. Time is the song's own clock at the written tempo, whatever the speed. The speed resets to 100% for a different song and comes back when the same song is reopened in the session. A song is known by the SHA-256 of its bytes (`DocumentFile.key`), never by its name, and a save registers the saved bytes' hash as the same song, so a saved file reopened keeps its speed.
- **Master volume** is `settings.masterVolume` and `audio.setMasterVolume(volume)` (0 to 1, on the sampler's master bus, so it scales the metronome too). WP-052's mixer binds the same field and method.
- **Preferences** (count-in, metronome on and volume, master volume) persist in `localStorage` under `reprise.transport`; WP-060 moves them into Dexie's `settings` table. Tempo and loop are per song and are not persisted.

## Seeking

Every seek goes through `seekTo(services, tick)` (`seek-commands.ts`), which asks the score to follow the playhead and then calls `audio.seek`. The audio service ignores a seek until the engine has the open song, which waits on the sound bank, so a click in the first seconds after opening does nothing rather than throw.

- **Which pass of a repeated bar a click lands on** (`seekTick` in `seek.ts`): the bar's pass in the playhead's _run_ (the stretch of consecutive passes that play consecutive bars), else the next pass after the playhead, else the last pass before it. Take bars 1 to 4 repeated, then 5 and 6, which play as 1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4 5 6. During the first time through, with the playhead in bar 3, a click on bar 2 goes back to bar 2 of the first time through, where "the next pass at or after the playhead" would skip ahead to the second time through and lose the repeat the player wanted to hear again. During the second time through, and from bar 6, whose run is 1 2 3 4 5 6 of the second time, it goes to the second time's bar 2. A click ahead, say on bar 5 during the first time through, takes the next pass after the playhead. The last pass before covers a bar that only played earlier, such as a first-time ending once its repeat is done. A bar no pass plays (an unused alternate ending) does not seek and is announced.
- **The playhead is the one drawn.** While playing, the drawn playhead lags the engine's clock by the output latency (see the score feature's README), and the pass rule and the steps decide from it (`services.heard`, which the driver writes every frame), so a click just after a repeat lands where the user saw the line, not where the engine already is.
- **With a loop on**, a click inside the loop's bars picks among the passes inside the engine's loop range, so it stays in the loop. A click outside them turns the loop off and seeks there.
- **Keyboard steps** (`stepTick`) walk the playback order, so they go through repeats as the music does: ← and → step between the current track's onsets in the bar (its first voice, or every voice when the first is empty), ⌘← and ⌘→ between bar passes, Home to the bar's start, ⌘Home and ⌘End to the first and last pass. While playing, ← steps past the sounding beat, so holding it does not restart the same beat. A step that cannot move does nothing. While stopped or paused each step announces the new position; while playing it stays silent. ⇧⌘← and ⇧⌘→ move the end of the bar selection away from its anchor and announce the range; the anchor is Shift+click's, the bar last clicked or selected from, else the playhead's bar.
- **Arrows, Home and End stay with a text field** whatever the modifiers, and with a control that handles them (sliders, menus), which prevents them before the window's dispatcher sees them.
